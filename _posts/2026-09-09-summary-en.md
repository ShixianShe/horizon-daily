## [OpenAI Claims AI Proof of Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) ⭐️ 10.0/10

On September 8, 2026, OpenAI announced that an internal AI system produced a proof showing that solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations can develop singularities in finite time, along with a formalization in the Lean proof assistant. The claim addresses the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the Millennium Prize Problems, but has not yet been verified by external mathematicians. If verified, this would be the first AI-generated solution to a Millennium Prize Problem, marking a milestone in AI-driven mathematical research. It could also accelerate progress in understanding fluid dynamics and turbulence, with implications for physics and engineering. OpenAI stated it will not claim the $1 million Clay Millennium Prize. The proof builds on a method by Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa (2023) for related fluid equations, and there is a priority dispute with researchers Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster, who derived closely related results on Euler equations. The announcement also includes a controversial sentence about de-identified data usage.

hackernews · tedsanders · Sep 8, 17:13 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49613262)

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem is one of seven Millennium Prize Problems set by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, each with a $1 million prize. It asks whether smooth, globally defined solutions always exist for the 3D Navier-Stokes equations, which describe fluid motion. The problem is central to understanding turbulence, a long-standing challenge in physics. Lean is a proof assistant used to formally verify mathematical proofs.

NeurIPS is a top machine learning conference, and desk rejection is a summary rejection without peer review, typically used for clear policy violations.

IPP (Internet Printing Protocol) is the modern standard for printer communication, allowing clients to discover and use printers without vendor-specific drivers.

## [LLMs Develop Novel Social Biases via Adaptive Exploration](https://openreview.net/challenge?redirect=%2Fforum%3Fid%3Dpc7fqaOcAH) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A study demonstrates that large language models (LLMs) can spontaneously develop novel social biases about artificial demographic groups through adaptive exploration, even when no inherent differences exist between the groups. This finding is significant for AI fairness research as it reveals that LLMs can create biases beyond those present in training data, potentially leading to unfair outcomes in real-world applications. It highlights the need for robust bias mitigation strategies that account for emergent biases during interaction. The study used a hiring consultant scenario with four artificial demographic groups (Tufa, Aima, Reku, Weki) and twenty different jobs, where the LLM received immediate feedback on hiring decisions. The results showed that LLMs developed preferences for certain groups over others, despite no actual performance differences, indicating the emergence of bias through adaptive exploration.

hackernews · paimapi · Sep 8, 21:47 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49617581)

**Background**: Large language models are trained on vast text corpora and can exhibit social biases present in the training data. However, this study shows that even when such biases are absent, LLMs can develop new biases through reinforcement learning-like processes where they adapt based on feedback. Adaptive exploration is a technique where an agent balances exploring new options and exploiting known good ones, which in this context led to the emergence of biased behavior.

OpenAI has introduced ChatGPT Images 2.5, an upgraded image generation model that improves multi-turn instruction following, speeds up response times, and better preserves subjects in reference photos. The update adds two new API model IDs: gpt-image-2.5-sunburst and gpt-image-2.5-flare. This release is significant because it addresses key pain points in AI image generation—consistent editing across multiple turns and subject preservation—which are critical for professional workflows. The availability of two distinct API models allows developers to choose between editing precision and speed, potentially broadening the adoption of AI-generated imagery in applications. The two new models are differentiated by their strengths: Sunburst is optimized for editing precision, while Flare is designed for fast, high-quality everyday generation. Simon Willison, the author of the post, upgraded his openai_image.py CLI tool to support reference images, demonstrating practical usage with the Sunburst model.

This initiative could significantly expand access to AI education by pairing each volunteer teacher with 10 students, potentially reaching a large audience. The course includes interactive tools and assignments, such as building an AI text detection app after about an hour of learning. Volunteer teachers receive training and practice with teachable agents, and the course is free thanks to funding from an alumnus.

## [Debate: Copyright Does More Harm Than Good, Should Be Abolished](https://grapheneos.social/@GrapheneOS/117231186011306184) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A Hacker News discussion argues that copyright in its current form does more harm than good and should be abolished, sparking debate on alternatives and the impact on creators. This debate challenges the foundations of intellectual property law, which underpins much of the software and creative industries. The outcome of such discussions could influence future policy and the balance between creator rights and public access. Commenters propose shorter copyright terms, such as two years, arguing that most revenue is earned early. Others distinguish between copyright itself and specific mechanisms like the DMCA, which they see as the real problem.

hackernews · Cider9986 · Sep 9, 06:31 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49622042)

**Background**: Copyright is a legal framework granting creators exclusive rights to their works for a limited time, intended to incentivize creation. Critics argue that overly long terms, such as life plus 70 years, stifle creativity and hinder access to culture, while proponents see it as essential for protecting creators' livelihoods.

**Discussion**: The community is divided: some agree that copyright's current form is harmful, citing its length and abuse, while others argue that the problem lies with specific enforcement mechanisms like the DMCA, not copyright itself. There is also a view that fields like FOSS and memes thrive precisely because they are less encumbered by copyright.
